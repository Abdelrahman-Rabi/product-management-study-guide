# Product Management Glossary

This glossary contains the main Product Management terms used throughout the
study guide.

The glossary should grow progressively as lectures are completed.

Each important term may include:

- A concise definition
- Related concepts
- Links to relevant lectures or summaries

Do not add terms before they appear in the course material.

---

## A

### Agile

An iterative approach to product or software development based on collaboration,
incremental delivery, feedback, and adaptation.

Agile delivery alone does not necessarily make the complete product-development
process Agile. Product planning and discovery may still follow a sequential
model.

**Related concepts:**

- Waterfall
- Product delivery
- Product discovery
- Iterative development

---

## B

### Business Case

An evaluation used to estimate the expected value, cost, benefits, and risks of
a product idea or initiative.

Early business cases often contain significant uncertainty because the final
solution, adoption level, and implementation complexity may not yet be known.

**Related concepts:**

- Prioritization
- Product roadmap
- Assumption
- Estimation

---

## D

### Digital Product

A software-based, often cloud-running value proposition (such as an application, API, or dataset) that solves user problems.

Unlike physical goods, a digital product is abstract and typically deployed to remote servers or cloud infrastructure.

**Related concepts:**
- Product decomposition
- Product lifecycle

**Related lectures:**
- [What's a Product?](./01-introduction/01-introduction/07-whats-a-product.md)

---

## O

### Outcome

A measurable customer, product, or business change created by a product
initiative.

Examples include:

- Increasing activation
- Reducing customer effort
- Improving retention
- Reducing recruiter review time
- Increasing successful task completion

**Related concepts:**

- Output
- Product goal
- Product metric
- Outcome roadmap

### Output

Something a team builds, delivers, or releases.

Examples include:

- A dashboard
- A mobile application
- An integration
- A reporting module
- An AI feature

An output is not automatically valuable. It becomes meaningful when it creates
a useful outcome.

**Related concepts:**

- Outcome
- Feature
- Delivery
- Roadmap

---

## P

### Problem Space

The customer goals, needs, pain points, behaviors, context, and constraints that
a product team seeks to understand.

The problem space focuses on:

- Who experiences the problem
- What they are trying to achieve
- What prevents success
- How frequently the problem occurs
- Why existing alternatives are insufficient

**Related concepts:**

- Solution space
- Customer research
- Product discovery
- Problem validation

### Product Decomposition

The practice of dividing a complex, large-scale software system into self-contained sub-products with individual ownership.

Decomposition allows separate teams and Product Managers to run development streams in parallel while maintaining a single, integrated user experience.

**Related concepts:**
- Digital Product
- Modular Ownership
- Product Manager (PM)

**Related lectures:**
- [What's a Product?](./01-introduction/01-introduction/07-whats-a-product.md)

### Product Discovery

The process of investigating customer problems, assumptions, risks, and
possible solutions before or alongside full product delivery.

Discovery helps teams reduce uncertainty before committing major time, money,
and engineering effort.

**Related concepts:**

- Validation
- Assumption
- Experiment
- Product delivery
- Problem space
- Solution space

### Product Delivery

The process of building, testing, releasing, and operating a product or feature.

Delivery focuses on turning a selected solution into reliable working software.

**Related concepts:**

- Product discovery
- Agile
- Development
- Release
- Quality assurance

### Product Lifecycle

The stages a product moves through from idea, introduction, and growth, to maturity and eventual decline.

**Related concepts:**

- Product-Market Fit (PMF)
- Product strategy

**Related lectures:**

- [What Does a PM Do?](./01-introduction/01-introduction/03-what-does-a-pm-do.md)

### Product Manager (PM)

The role responsible for driving a product from inception to delivery by owning the "what" and "why" decisions.

**Related concepts:**

- Product lifecycle
- Product-Market Fit (PMF)

**Related lectures:**

- [What Does a PM Do?](./01-introduction/01-introduction/03-what-does-a-pm-do.md)

### Product-Market Fit (PMF)

The sweet spot where a product successfully solves a critical problem for a customer base that is willing to buy it.

**Related concepts:**

- Product lifecycle
- Product discovery
- Validation

**Related lectures:**

- [What Does a PM Do?](./01-introduction/01-introduction/03-what-does-a-pm-do.md)

### Product Roadmap

A communication tool that explains product direction, priorities, and intended
areas of investment over time.

A roadmap may focus on:

- Features and projects
- Problems to solve
- Goals
- Outcomes
- Strategic priorities

**Related concepts:**

- Feature roadmap
- Outcome roadmap
- Prioritization
- Product strategy

---

## S

### Solution Space

The possible ways a product team may address a validated problem or achieve a
desired outcome.

The solution space may include:

- Product workflows
- User experience options
- Technical approaches
- Prototypes
- Experiments
- Alternative implementation paths

**Related concepts:**

- Problem space
- Product discovery
- Prototyping
- Technical feasibility

---

## V

### Validation

The process of collecting evidence to determine whether an assumption about a
problem, solution, market, or user behavior is likely to be correct.

Validation does not remove all uncertainty. It reduces the risk of making a
large investment based only on assumptions.

**Related concepts:**

- Product discovery
- Experiment
- Evidence
- Assumption
- Learning

---

## W

### Waterfall

A sequential approach in which work progresses through predefined stages such
as:

```text
Ideas
  ↓
Business Case
  ↓
Roadmap
  ↓
Requirements
  ↓
Design
  ↓
Development
  ↓
Testing
  ↓
Release

In a waterfall process, feedback and validation often arrive after substantial
work has already been completed.

Related concepts:

Agile
Sequential development
Late validation
Product delivery
