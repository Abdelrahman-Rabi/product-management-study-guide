# Section 1: Introduction

## Section Overview

This section covers the foundations of the Product Management role and how modern product teams operate. It defines the core responsibilities of a Product Manager (PM), explains what a digital product is, contrasts feature-delivery teams with empowered product teams, and describes the collaborative relationship between PMs, Product Designers, and Tech Leads. By understanding these concepts, the learner will build a strong foundation in the product operating model before diving into specific discovery and strategy techniques.

## Learning Goals

After completing this section, the learner should understand:
- The distinction between a Product Manager's collaborative influence and a people manager's hierarchical authority.
- The definition of a digital product and how to scope a product's boundaries.
- The four core product risks (Value, Usability, Feasibility, and Viability) and which roles in the product triad are responsible for them.
- The differences between output-driven delivery teams and outcome-driven empowered product teams.
- How to transform developers into missionary co-creators of solutions rather than mercenary feature-builders.

## Key Concepts

- **Product Triad:** The collaborative peer relationship between Product Manager, Product Designer, and Tech Lead.
- **Product Risks:** Value risk, Usability risk, Feasibility risk, and Viability risk.
- **Empowered Product Teams:** Cross-functional teams that are given business problems to solve and measured by outcomes.
- **Missionary Developer:** An engineer who understands customer context, experiences user pain, and co-creates solutions.
- **Dual-Track Agile:** Running discovery (learning) and delivery (building) in parallel.
