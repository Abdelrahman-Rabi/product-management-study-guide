# Learning Mission: Product Management

## Why

Build practical Product Management judgment from the course so the learner can understand, review, and apply its ideas without repeatedly rewatching lectures.

## Success Looks Like

- Explain the course's central Product Management mental models clearly.
- Diagnose product situations before jumping to feature solutions.
- Make defensible decisions across strategy, discovery, design, delivery, and measurement.
- Connect Product Management reasoning to software development, APIs, data, architecture, and technical constraints when relevant.
- Navigate from a lecture to its prerequisites, applications, and extensions across the course.

## Learner Context

- The learner has a software-development background.
- Technical analogies can reduce explanation cost when they clarify rather than replace Product Management reasoning.
- Familiarity with software delivery does not imply mastery of Product Management discovery, strategy, influence, or decision-making.

## Constraints

- Preserve the supplied transcript as the only lecture-content source.
- Prefer focused, practical explanations over exhaustive expansion.
- Preserve the original course hierarchy and sequence.
- Treat existing study-guide coverage as exposure, not evidence of learner mastery.
- Add quizzes, exercises, reflection questions, or assignments only when explicitly requested.

## Out of Scope

- Replacing the original course structure with a topic-based curriculum.
- Adding external frameworks or research to canonical lecture content without an explicit enrichment request.
- Tracking learner mastery without evidence from the learner.
