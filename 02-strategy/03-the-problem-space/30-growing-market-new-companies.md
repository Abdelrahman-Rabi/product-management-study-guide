# Growing Market = New Companies

> **Material level:** Level 2 — Core lecture exploring how to evaluate market growth using the three growth lenses, and analyzing why selling B2B products to new market entrants (startups) is strategically superior to targeting established enterprises.

## Lecture Overview

Product managers must build strategies around growing markets. This lecture introduces a framework for diagnosing market growth and explores the distinct buyer behaviors of new entrants vs. established companies. By the end of this lecture, you should be able to:
* Evaluate any market using the three growth lenses: Prevalence, Frequency, and Severity.
* Explain the strategic risks of stable or declining markets.
* Contrast the B2B buying behavior of new companies vs. old companies across four dimensions.
* Detail why selling to new entrants is the primary driver of rapid product growth.

---

## 1. The Three Growth Lenses

To determine if a target opportunity space is growing or shrinking, ask yourself three questions looking five years into the future:

1.  **Prevalence:** *Will more people have this problem?* (Audience expansion)
2.  **Frequency:** *Will people have this problem more often?* (Usage intensity)
3.  **Severity:** *Will the problem hurt more?* (Financial or operational pain size)

If the answer is **"Less"** for any of these lenses, the market is shrinking. When entering a shrinking market, the fundamental value of your product declines before you write a single line of code.

## 2. Why Target Growing Markets?

While it is tempting to build for a stable, mature market, growth is critical for startup success. Growing markets generate **new entrants** (new companies, projects, or departments). 

In B2B product strategy, **selling to new companies is always superior to selling to old, established companies.**

### Buyer Persona Matrix: New Entrants vs. Established Companies

![New vs. Old Companies B2B Buyer Matrix](./visuals/30-new-vs-old-buyers.png)
*B2B buyer behavior grid: contrasting startup new entrants with legacy enterprise buyers.*

| Dimension | New Entrant (Startup / New Company) | Established Entity (Enterprise / Old Company) |
|---|---|---|
| **Adaptability** | **Adapts to your tool.** Stacks their workflows around your software's default design. | **Demands your tool adapt to them.** Requires custom integrations, custom fields, and consulting hours. |
| **Problem Urgency** | **Burning Need.** You are their first solution; without you, they cannot function. | **Optimization Problem.** They already have an existing workaround; they are just seeking small efficiency gains. |
| **Risk Profile** | **Willing to take bets.** Open to trying unproven software from early-stage startups. | **Established patterns.** Risk-averse; requires extensive case studies, references, and industry compliance. |
| **Sales Velocity** | **Fast procurement.** Quick decisions made by founders; minimal administrative friction. | **Long sales cycles.** Slow purchasing committees, legal checks, security audits, and RFP processes. |

## Practical B2B Product Example

### Situation
A PM is building a **B2B carbon accounting software**. They are choosing between two target customer profiles:
1.  **Profile A (Established Manufacturing Giants):** Multi-billion dollar industrial corporations that have done manual spreadsheet carbon accounting for 15 years.
2.  **Profile B (Newly Created Clean-Tech Startups):** Venture-backed green energy startups that need to report emissions to investors immediately to secure their next round of funding.

### Decision
The PM targets Profile B (New Entrants) for their initial launch.

### Reasoning
*   **Adaptability:** Profile B has no legacy workflow. They will sign up and use the tool as-is. Profile A has custom ERP databases and demands that the carbon tool build custom connectors to their legacy software.
*   **Urgency:** Profile B has a burning need (funding milestones). Profile A treats carbon accounting as a compliance chore to optimize.
*   **Sales Cycle:** Profile B signs up using a self-serve credit card portal. Profile A requires a 12-month security audit and procurement review.

### Consequence
By focusing on Profile B, the PM closes 50 customers in 3 months. When they have achieved scale and product maturity, they leverage these case studies to target Profile A, winning enterprise accounts with a much more robust product.

## Common Misunderstandings

### "Old companies have bigger budgets, so we should target them first"
**Correction:** While enterprises have high purchasing power, the cost-to-serve (custom integrations, long sales cycles, support overhead) often wipes out the margin. Startups buy faster and adapt to your product, allowing you to iterate rapidly on your core features.

### "Stable markets are safe because there is no competitive chaos"
**Correction:** Stable markets have zero new entrants. This means you can only acquire customers by stealing them from established competitors (churn battles). In contrast, growing B2B markets provide a steady stream of new buyers who do not have an existing solution, dramatically lowering your customer acquisition cost (CAC).

## Key Concepts and Definitions

| Concept | Simple definition |
|---|---|
| **Prevalence** | The total number of people or businesses experiencing a specific problem in a target market. |
| **Frequency** | How often a target user experiences the pain of the problem (e.g., daily vs. once a year). |
| **Severity** | The intensity, financial cost, or operational damage caused by the problem if left unsolved. |
| **New Entrant Advantage** | The strategic speed advantage gained by selling to newly formed buyers who have no legacy tool locks. |

---

## Key Takeaways

1. **Pick a growing market** where prevalence, frequency, or severity will increase over the next 5 10 years.
2. **Avoid shrinking markets** where value declines before product development even begins.
3. **Growing B2B markets** provide new entrants, which are the easiest customers to sell to.
4. **New companies adapt** to your workflows; old companies require your tool to adapt to them.
5. **Startups seek first solutions** for burning needs; enterprises seek optimization.
6. **New buyers make fast decisions;** established companies have long, slow procurement cycles.
7. **Selling solely to old companies** slows down growth due to high custom development demands.

## One-Minute Review

*   **Audit Growth Lenses:** Ensure Prevalence, Frequency, or Severity are expanding looking five years out.
*   **Focus B2B Startup Entrants:** Startups scale fast because they align their processes with your standardized SaaS software, bypass slow committees, and represent high urgency deals.
