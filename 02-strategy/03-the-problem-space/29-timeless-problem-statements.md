# Exercise #8: Timeless Problem Statements

> **Material level:** Level 2 — Hands-on exercise applying technology-agnostic and vendor-neutral framing rules to core product opportunity spaces and strategic vision statements.

## Exercise Overview

This exercise guides you through auditing and reframing product opportunity statements. Defining problems too closely to a specific **delivery technology** or **vendor partner** restricts long-term innovation and exposes your company to supplier risks. By completing this exercise, you will be able to:
* Identify technology and vendor dependencies in draft problem statements.
* Reframe locked statements into timeless, durable descriptions of customer value.
* Analyze high-level product vision statements (e.g., Uber) for strategic resilience.
* Audit your own product's vision statement using a two-step dependency test.

---

## Part A: Evaluating Warm-Up Problem Statements

To understand how to identify strategic dependencies, evaluate the following four common examples of locked problem statements and their reframed, timeless solutions:

![Evaluating Problem Statements](./visuals/29-problem-evaluations.png)
*Warm-up statement evaluations: identifying specific medium or provider locks and reframing to optimized solutions.*

### Statement 1: "I want to swap phone numbers via Bluetooth with people nearby."
*   **The Dependency:** Both "phone numbers" and "Bluetooth" are transient delivery channels. We cannot predict whether Bluetooth as a protocol standard or phone numbers as identifiers will remain dominant 20 years from now.
*   **The Reframe:** *"I want to swap my contact details with people nearby."* 
    *   *Why it is timeless:* Abstractions like "contact details" allow the tech stack to shift from Bluetooth to NFC, QR codes, or biometric scans without changing the product strategy.

### Statement 2: "I wanna learn product management on Udemy."
*   **The Dependency:** "Udemy" is a single partner vendor. If you define your problem space or customer segment solely by one provider, you assume all of their company risks (pricing shifts, supplier policies, platform decay).
*   **The Reframe:** *"I want to acquire product management knowledge and skills."*
    *   *Why it is timeless:* Focusing on the core skill acquisition prevents you from being tightly coupled to a single learning supplier or platform context.

### Statement 3: "I want to hire a car on demand."
*   **The Dependency:** "Car" is a specific vehicle type and technology. While dominant today, the human need is not the metal vehicle itself, but the movement of bodies and goods.
*   **The Reframe:** *"I want to secure reliable, on-demand transportation."*
    *   *Why it is timeless:* If the delivery method transitions to self-driving pods, electric bikes, or drone corridors, this statement remains strategically valid.

### Statement 4: "I wanna send emails automatically when users achieve certain milestones in my web app."
*   **The Dependency:** "Emails" (delivery channel) and "web app" (device/platform medium) are locked technologies. Milestones can be flagged on smart glasses, watch screens, or automated chats.
*   **The Reframe:** *"I want to trigger contextual messages to users when they reach key usage milestones."*
    *   *Why it is timeless:* It allows the messaging system to transition from traditional email to push alerts, SMS, or direct system calls seamlessly.

## Part B: The Uber Vision Case Study

Uber's early strategic vision statement represents an excellent benchmark for technology-agnostic framing:

> **Uber Vision Statement:** *"Transportation as reliable as water everywhere for everyone."*

![Uber's Product Vision Breakdown](./visuals/29-uber-vision-breakdown.png)
*Uber Product Vision text annotations: dissecting utilities and proof of dependency neutrality.*

### Why this stands the test of time:
1.  **No Technology dependencies:** The statement does not mention cars, smartphone applications, cellular network GPS, or telephone dispatch operators.
2.  **No Vendor dependencies:** The company does not limit its supply chain partners or link itself to single-provider infrastructures.
3.  **Timeless Utility:** Moving people and goods (transportation) is a permanent human need.
4.  **Clear Standards:** Comparing accessibility to "water everywhere for everyone" establishes a high reliability and coverage goal without naming any physical delivery equipment.

## Common Misunderstandings

### "Technology-agnostic statements are too generic to guide daily sprint planning"
**Correction:** The strategic statement defines the *why* and the long-term *opportunity direction*. Daily sprint tasks must still be highly specific (e.g., "Build the Bluetooth sync handler"). Decoupled strategic definitions ensure that when engineers replace the Bluetooth sync with NFC in two years, the team does not have to redefine the product's business value.

### "Vendor-neutral statements ignore current business realities"
**Correction:** While your startup may launch exclusively on Shopify or Salesforce today, the *problem space* is Shopify-neutral. Framing the problem space as "managing customer order data" rather than "syncing Salesforce contact records" ensures that your strategic moat does not disappear if that vendor changes their developer terms or goes out of business.

## Key Concepts and Definitions

| Concept | Simple definition |
|---|---|
| **Technology Dependency** | A strategic flaw where a problem statement couples user objectives to specific transient hardware, software, or formats. |
| **Vendor Dependency** | A business risk where a product statement couples its opportunity space or market segment to a single external partner company. |
| **Agnostic Framing** | Abstracting requirements to focus on the underlying customer objective rather than specific delivery tools. |
| **Timeless Utility** | A fundamental customer need (e.g., communication, transit) that remains valid across generational technology shifts. |
