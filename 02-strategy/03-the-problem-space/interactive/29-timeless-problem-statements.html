Audit and reframe product problem statements and vision statements to ensure they stand the test of time.

Your goal

Analyze draft opportunity statements, detect hidden supplier and medium dependencies, and reframe them into timeless customer utility descriptions.

Exercise overview

This exercise guides you through auditing and reframing product opportunity statements. Defining problems too closely to a specific delivery technology or vendor partner restricts long-term innovation and exposes your company to supplier risks.

Part A: Evaluating Warm-Up Problem Statements

Review the four common examples of locked problem statements and their reframed solutions. Compare the dependencies on phone numbers, Bluetooth, Udemy, cars, and email/web apps.

Part B: The Uber Vision Case Study

Uber's early strategic vision statement represents an excellent benchmark for technology-agnostic framing: "Transportation as reliable as water everywhere for everyone." It names no vehicles, smartphone platforms, or cellular network dispatch software.

Part C: Vision Audit Worksheet

Audit your own product's vision or primary problem statement using these two questions. Decouple it from specific delivery technologies or companies. Focus entirely on the core human objective.

Common misunderstandings

"Technology-agnostic statements are too generic to guide daily sprint planning"

Correction: The strategic statement defines the why and the long-term opportunity direction. Daily sprint tasks must still be highly specific. Decoupled strategic definitions ensure that when engineers replace the Bluetooth sync with NFC in two years, the team does not have to redefine the product's business value.

"Vendor-neutral statements ignore current business realities"

Correction: While your startup may launch exclusively on Shopify or Salesforce today, the problem space is Shopify-neutral. Framing the problem space as "managing customer order data" rather than "syncing Salesforce contact records" ensures that your strategic moat does not disappear if that vendor changes their developer terms or goes out of business.

Key concepts and definitions

  • Technology Dependency: A strategic flaw where a problem statement couples user objectives to specific transient hardware, software, or formats.
  • Vendor Dependency: A business risk where a product statement couples its opportunity space or market segment to a single external partner company.
  • Agnostic Framing: Abstracting requirements to focus on the underlying customer objective rather than specific delivery tools.
  • Timeless Utility: A fundamental customer need (e.g., communication, transit) that remains valid across generational technology shifts.
